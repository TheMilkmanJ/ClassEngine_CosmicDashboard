# Can-exist — invariant mass of condensed light (steelman)

**Candidate IM-B:** system invariant mass \(M_\mathrm{inv}=\sqrt{E^2-p^2}\) for collective EM configurations.

## Why the premise is not nonsense

1. **Relativity:** multiparticle and bound systems have invariant mass even when constituents are massless (e.g. a photon gas in a box has rest energy in the box frame).  
2. **Condensed-light experiments:** cavity photon fluids and polariton condensates are described with rest-frame thermodynamics.  
3. **Hunt psychology:** many birefringence attempts tried to give photons a mass handle; IM-B is the cleanest *non-Proca* way to say “light has rest energy.”  
4. **Distinct from plasma mass:** does not require inventing vacuum \(m_\gamma\); does not automatically equal \(\omega_p\).

## What would have to be true for IM-B to *help* birefringence

Even on steelman, IM-B would need a **further bridge** (not supplied by \(M_\mathrm{inv}\) alone):

- a parity-odd effective index \(n_L\neq n_R\) for free-streaming CMB modes, **and**  
- a medium coupling that generates it without EM charge, **or**  
- an allowed charged middleman that is still “the medium” under L1.

Without that bridge, can-exist only supports “systems of light can have rest mass,” not “therefore \(\beta\neq0\).”
